# Positioning

The source of truth for public-facing identity. When writing anything that speaks as you to an
outside audience -- bio, case study, site copy, LinkedIn, the opening of a proposal -- read this
file. [[style/voice]] carries the voice; this file carries the canonical wording and the claim
boundaries.

## Claim boundaries

<!-- This is where you define what can and cannot be said on your behalf.

Some claims are fine on LinkedIn but not on an unattributed surface. Some are fine in a private
document but not in public copy. Define the tiers:

- **Tier 1 -- public, unattributed.** What can appear on a website, in a bio, or in copy where
  the reader does not know who wrote it. Only claims you can back with public evidence.
- **Tier 2 -- public, attributed.** What can appear on LinkedIn, in a byline, in a talk abstract.
  Broader than tier 1 because the reader can evaluate credibility.
- **Tier 3 -- private.** What can appear in a proposal, a client document, or an internal memo.
  Claims that require context the reader already has.

The boundaries are hard rules, not stylistic preferences. The assistant does not invent a title,
pillar, credential, or metric when writing anything that speaks publicly as you.
-->
